# Design Reference Protocol

## Why References Are Used

References provide evidence about patterns and interaction conventions. They do not authorize copying another product's brand, content, layout, assets, or visual identity.

## Reference Record

For every external reference, record:

- Source name and exact URL.
- Retrieval date.
- Product, platform, and surface when known.
- Pattern or state observed.
- What is directly observed.
- What principle is transferable.
- What must not be copied.
- Limitations, such as screenshot-only evidence or unavailable interaction context.

## Source Selection

- Use shipped product libraries for product UI and states.
- Use flow libraries for transitions and task sequences.
- Use curated galleries for visual composition and art direction.
- Use authoritative usability and accessibility guidance for behavior and inclusion.
- Cross-check a visual idea against the project's users, content, platform, and technical constraints.

## Anti-AI-Looking Gate

A design direction must explain its relationship to the project. Do not accept a direction based only on fashionable tokens or a reference screenshot.

Check for:

- coherent hierarchy and density;
- real or representative content;
- intentional states and feedback;
- restrained, meaningful decoration;
- typography and color chosen for the task;
- responsive and accessibility behavior;
- a clear reason for distinctive details.

## Evidence Status

Label reference claims as `Observed`, `Inferred`, `Proposed`, `Assumption`, or `Unverified`. A screenshot proves appearance at a moment; it does not prove usability, accessibility, performance, or behavior outside the captured state.
